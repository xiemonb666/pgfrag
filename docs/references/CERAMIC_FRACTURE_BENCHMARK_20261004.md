# Ceramic fracture benchmark evidence (2026-10-04)

## Finding

The closest coherent, pottery-relevant primary dataset found is the experimental Kalami-clay briquette series in Müller et al. (2015). It studies deliberately prepared, low-calcareous clay ceramics with documented firing, porosity, bending modulus, flexural strength, and notched-beam fracture tests. It is a useful partial comparator for PG-Frag; it is not a complete material card and is not a test of excavated sherds. In particular, Poisson ratio, a measured fired bulk density, support spans, and digitized load–displacement records are missing.

For a first scalar fracture check, the most complete condition is the **untempered Kalami clay fired at 1050 °C**. The paper calls it brittle, reports `E = 68.4 ± 7.5 GPa`, `K_Ic,eff ≈ 1.2 MPa·m^1/2`, and an unnotched transverse rupture strength (TRS) of about `70 MPa`; total porosity is about `11%`, mostly closed. The paper's relation `K_Ic = sqrt(E G_Ic)` gives an approximate `G_Ic,eff ≈ 21 J/m²` when those reported values are substituted. That is a calculation from rounded published values, not an independently measured `G_c`. This 1050 °C material is strongly vitrified and unusually stiff relative to low-fired pottery; it must remain a distinct high-fire comparator.

The same study offers a more porous, lower-fired Kalami condition at 850 °C, with `E = 5.5 ± 0.8 GPa` and total porosity about `32 ± 2%`. This is closer to an earthenware-like elastic comparator, but the paper does not give a directly usable numeric `K_Ic`/`G_Ic` for that specific condition in its text or tables. Do not transfer the 1050 °C fracture value to it. This observation is especially relevant to the current PG prototype: its `E = 5 GPa` happens to be near the 850 °C study mean, but that numerical proximity does not validate PG's material card or its other parameters.

## Candidate numeric condition: Kalami low-calcareous clay, untempered, 1050 °C

| Property | Evidence and status |
|---|---|
| Material and preparation | Experimental briquettes made from a `<30 μm` fraction of low-calcareous Kalami clay from Crete (`<0.5% CaO`), with no added temper for this condition. The starting paste was moulded at about `120 × 70 × 10 mm` under approximately `0.02 MPa`, dried for over 10 days under plaster plates, then fired in air at `200 °C/h` with a 1 h soak. Source: pp. 832–833. |
| Fired test-bar geometry | Approximately `10 × 10 × 70 mm`; test bars were cut from the fired briquette. Source: p. 833. |
| Density | **Fired bulk density: MISSING.** The authors report estimated true density of the Kalami raw material as `2.77 ± 0.05 g/ml`, based on constituent mineral phases; do not relabel that as measured fired bulk density. The untempered 1050 °C condition has about `11%` total porosity, mainly closed. Source: pp. 833, 835 and Fig. 3. |
| Young's modulus | `68.4 ± 7.5 GPa`, mean of five independent compliance-corrected load–displacement determinations. Source: Table 1, p. 838. |
| Poisson ratio | **MISSING.** |
| Flexural strength | TRS is reported as about `70 MPa` for untempered low-calcareous clay fired at 1050 °C. It is a flaw-sensitive three-point-bend strength, not an intrinsic tensile strength. Five measurements were used per material condition; bars with visible macroscopic flaws were excluded. Source: pp. 834, 836. The support span is **MISSING**, so the strength test cannot be reproduced exactly from the paper alone. |
| Notched fracture test | Four-point bending of pre-notched bars, about `10 × 10 × 70 mm`; notch depth `1 ± 0.1 mm`, cut with a `0.15 mm` diamond wafering blade; crosshead speed `54 μm/min`; three bars per material condition. K was evaluated at peak load with a finite-notch correction. Sources: pp. 834–835. Outer/inner support spans and raw per-specimen curves are **MISSING** from the article text. |
| Fracture toughness | `K_Ic,eff ≈ 1.2 MPa·m^1/2` for the highest-fired, untempered low-calcareous specimens, described as brittle. This is the authors' effective value, not a separately established ASTM plane-strain `K_IC`. Source: p. 837. |
| Fracture energy | The authors state `K_Ic = sqrt(E G_Ic)`. Applying that paper convention to the rounded values above gives `G_Ic,eff ≈ K²/E = (1.2×10^6 Pa·√m)²/(68.4×10^9 Pa) ≈ 21 J/m²`. It is **derived here**, not quoted as a measured energy value. With an isotropic LEFM convention, plane stress uses `G = K²/E`; plane strain uses `G = (1−ν²)K²/E`. Since ν is missing and the paper does not establish a plane-strain size condition, the plane-strain value cannot be evaluated or claimed. |
| Stable-growth energy | The selected untempered 1050 °C condition is reported as brittle. A post-peak stable-growth component `G_t` is therefore not reported for it; the method assigns no stable-tail contribution in the unstable-fracture regime. This must not be mistaken for an independent measurement of zero total fracture energy. |
| Crack image / scale | No digitized crack path, shard set, or measured process-zone image for this exact untempered test condition was found in the paper. Fig. 2 documents matrix microstructure by firing condition; the paper's crack-surface imagery is for other recipes (see qualitative-only evidence below). |

### Minimal local numerical comparison supported by this condition

The narrowest defensible case is an elastic–brittle, mode-I notched-beam initiation check using the reported `E`, the approximate `K_Ic,eff`, and the paper-convention derived `G_Ic,eff`. Match the reported 10 mm square section, 70 mm length, and 1 mm edge notch; represent four-point loading only after obtaining support positions from the original authors or another primary record. If spans remain unavailable, label the run a geometry-informed scalar check rather than an experimental replay. Compare crack initiation/energy scale only; the paper supplies no digitized load curve or validated 3-D crack trajectory.

An 850 °C untempered Kalami case can check only the elastic range (`E = 5.5 ± 0.8 GPa`) and porous-body behavior (`P_total ≈ 32 ± 2%`). It cannot check crack initiation without a condition-matched toughness value. The 1050 °C toughness must not be carried over.

The papers provide no measured fracture-process-zone length or mapping to a peridynamic horizon. Consequently, `δ` cannot be inferred from the plotted energy, set equal to the notch width, or tuned until one curve looks similar. Treat the numerical horizon/discretization as a numerical parameter requiring its own convergence study, separate from this material evidence.

## Qualitative fracture-surface evidence only

Kilikoglou et al. (1998), Fig. 10, p. 273, shows a stereo-SEM pair of a fresh four-point-bend fracture surface for a separate recipe: `20 vol%` quartz temper, mean grain size `450 μm`. The authors describe a step-like surface. The caption does not give a calibrated image scale or a digitized crack trace. It can support only a qualitative comparison of fracture-surface relief/crack deflection. It is a different clay recipe and temper fraction from the Kalami numeric condition above, so none of its appearance or material parameters should be merged into that condition.

The same 1998 article, Fig. 2, pp. 265–266, plots illustrative load–displacement responses for 5% and 40% quartz at `250 μm` mean grain size, distinguishing unstable failure from stable crack growth. Those are plotted examples, not an openly tabulated time series. Use them to classify response shape qualitatively, not as a calibrated raw curve. The article's test details (for its own recipe) include `10 × 10 × 60 mm` bars, about `2 mm` notch depth and `50 μm/min` loading; these belong to the 1998 recipe and are not the dimensions for the 2015 Kalami case.

## Qualification for PG-Frag

This evidence is strong enough to stop treating a single unverified parameter set as pottery-calibrated: a primary study reports fired-clay moduli from `5.5 GPa` (Kalami, 850 °C, untempered) to `68.4 GPa` (same clay, 1050 °C, untempered), depending on firing, alongside large porosity changes. It does **not** validate the present PG prototype values `E=5 GPa`, `ν=0.24`, `ρ=2100 kg/m³`, `G_c=45 J/m²`, or `δ=3 mm` as one coherent ceramic card. The numeric candidate is high-fired experimental pottery clay, with ν, fired bulk density, exact fixture spans, and raw curves absent; it is an initial comparison target whose adoption and numerical interpretation remain for the main agent to decide.
