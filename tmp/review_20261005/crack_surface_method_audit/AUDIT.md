# PG-Frag crack-surface method audit

**Scope.** Read-only source and primary-literature review for the gap from PG-Frag native broken-bond data to paired geometric crack surfaces and a RelicWeave observed-fragment query. No solver, CAD, tests, or source changes were made. This note is a method proposal, not a fracture result.

## Finding

The current evidence stops before geometric fracture output. PG-Frag's production slice is a bounded bar fixture; it records point and horizon-bond states, but it does not emit a crack mesh, paired surfaces, dynamic fragment geometry, contact, or historical A12 adjacency. Its `forge.asset.v1` validation is only a package-contract check, and `run` returns `UNSUPPORTED` because surface-to-volume discretization and void/topology checks are absent (`pgfrag/README.md:6-19`, `pgfrag/src/pgfrag/backend.py:95-99, 461-484`).

A newer free-fracture pilot source adds canonical native bond pairs, first active-to-inactive events, time-indexed state, and active-graph component labels. It remains `FROZEN_SOURCE_ONLY` / `SOURCE_ONLY_NOT_RUN`; its case is a homogeneous 4,500-point analytic box at `h=0.6 mm`, `delta=1.8 mm`, OSB `NoCorrection`, and uncalibrated material inputs. The source review counts only 25.2% of box points with a complete horizon and leaves free-surface bias open. It explicitly disclaims physical fragments and crack surfaces (`pgfrag/experiments/free_fracture_pilot_20261005/fixed_case.toml:16-37,81-82`; `pgfrag/tmp/review_20261005/free_fracture_pilot/SOURCE_REVIEW.md:3-8,38-53`). It also carries no measured ceramic grain, pore, or flaw field. This pilot could improve event evidence, but it does not supply a surface representation or identify one from the graph; a rough result from it would not establish resolved, material-realistic roughness.

The v1 RelicWeave reader is also intentionally bounded: it can verify the endpoint tables and hashes, then reports `PG_DIAGNOSTIC_ONLY`; physical fragments, crack surfaces, per-step event history, and historical R2 remain `UNKNOWN` (`relicweave-synlab/synlab/qa/source_readers/pgfrag_result.py:1-5,593-596,829`). The copied endpoint fixture says the same (`relicweave-synlab/tests/fixtures/pgfrag/result_v1_toy_endpoint/README.md`). The free-fracture pilot's event archive is not yet part of that result schema.

The geometry ingress gap precedes fracture: VesselForge's package carries a triangular surface and may declare a STEP solid, but the current SynLab reader stores the surface arrays and only checks that the optional STEP file is present; it does not parse it into a volume mesh (`relicweave-synlab/synlab/integrations/forge_asset.py:49-73,334-343`). A true hollow vessel therefore needs a validated material-domain discretization, explicit inner/outer boundary and void handling, point volumes, and an initial interaction policy that prevents horizon bonds from bridging empty space. Retaining points inside a box around the vessel would not provide those semantics.

SynLab already has a separate, useful downstream precedent: its procedural shared-cut backend makes both sides from one discrete cutter and pairs clipped interface faces; clean truth binds interfaces to fragment pairs and keeps historical R2 separate from later degradation (`relicweave-synlab/synlab/fracture/event.py:206-217,364-375`; `relicweave-synlab/synlab/truth/api.py:522-625,894-899`). Those interfaces have explicit geometry and source lineage. They are not reconstructed from PG-Frag broken bonds, and the current `forge_asset` reader rejects reserved face region 255 without fracture-event lineage (`.../integrations/forge_asset.py:538-545`).

## Why the bond graph does not determine a unique surface

A PG-Frag bond event gives endpoint IDs, their coordinates, and a break time. It does not say where a continuous crack surface lies between the endpoints, which part of a diffuse damage zone is the crack, or how to pair two surface sides. The horizon is nonlocal, so multiple broken bonds can span a crack/process zone; the same partition of material points is compatible with many triangulations and sub-point surface locations. First-break timestamps add history but do not resolve this geometric ambiguity. If surviving bonds still connect points across a locally damaged region, active-graph components do not even define two sides of that partial crack.

This is consistent with the literature, not a PG-Frag-specific quirk. Diehl et al.'s particle-fragment visualization uses component labeling with a damage threshold and maximum bond length; it describes the result as a visualization and notes sensitivity to initial point placement. It is a useful precedent for graph-based fragment proxies, not a paired crack-surface method ([Diehl et al., 2015, DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-319-51954-8_2), [author PDF](https://freysn.github.io/papers/extraction_peri.pdf), especially pp. 5–7, 15). Giannakeas et al.'s coupled XFEM–PD paper states that bonds cannot be directly related to geometric information and estimates the crack tip from a damage field instead ([Giannakeas et al., 2020, DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00466-020-01872-8), §4). Regular peridynamic grids can also favor crack paths aligned with bond directions; Dipasquale et al. demonstrate grid-orientation dependence and weaker directions ([Dipasquale et al., 2016, DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.engfracmech.2016.03.022)).

Thus, neither broken-bond count nor component count is crack area, topology, physical-fragment truth, or R2. For this pilot's `delta/h = 3`, surface detail below the material-point spacing and horizon scale is unresolved. Smoothing or adding visual roughness cannot recover it; that detail must be reported as unknown or represented as an uncertainty band.

## Implementable paths

| Path | Method and output semantics | Prerequisites | Relative cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Cell-interface reconstruction** | Create a body-conforming tetrahedral or clipped-cell discretization, retain its material-cell adjacency, and label cells from the final active-bond components. Extract shared dual-cell facets between components and duplicate/orient them as the two sides of an interface. Associate each patch with nearby first-break bond IDs as supporting provenance. Emit `RECONSTRUCTION_PROXY`; do not promote it to physical crack GT or R2. This can produce usable geometry when the solver has fully separated components. It cannot recover a still-connected partial crack without an additional front/sides field. | A verified 3-D material volume, point-to-cell/cell-volume mapping, void-safe initial horizon graph, and a stable mapping from cells to native point IDs. For the first vessel, use an explicitly verified solid/volume representation; a surface-only STL/NPZ is not enough. | Medium post-processing cost; the expensive/new work is volume discretization, point quadrature, and void-safe neighbor construction. Facet extraction is roughly proportional to cells/facets and graph edges. No measured runtime is available. |
| **2. Track explicit crack surfaces during fracture** | Extend or replace the current OSB point-history path with an explicit crack-front/surface state: create nonplanar facets as cracks nucleate/advance, preserve each interface ID and side, and record the first-break events and material points that support each update. Partition the volume with those interfaces to make fragments. This is the only path here that makes simulated paired surfaces explicit state rather than a post-hoc choice. Even then, “physical GT” requires calibrated material behavior and validation against measured fracture surfaces. | A volume model and void-safe interactions; crack-front/surface tracking coupled to failure; a rule for branching, junctions, and front termination; side-specific displacement interpolation; contact/separation handling; calibration and experimental reference data. The cracking-particle method is a relevant research pattern, but it is a distinct formulation, not a drop-in Peridynamics.jl OSB feature ([Ai & Augarde, 2018, DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/nme.5786)). | High. This changes solver state and fracture formulation and adds per-step surface/front work, surface correspondence, and additional validation. It is an architectural fork. |
| **3. Damage-field isosurface** | Interpolate point damage or local broken-bond density onto a body-masked grid and extract one or more marching-cubes contours. Use only as `VISUALIZATION_PROXY` for viewing a diffuse damage band. It does not inherently produce a complementary surface pair, a fragment partition, or historical adjacency. | Point positions and scalar damage; body/void mask; an explicit threshold and interpolation/resolution metadata. | Low-to-medium post-processing; grid memory and output grow with volumetric resolution. Cheap relative to new solver tracking, but threshold-dependent and not suitable for GT. |

Path 1 is the smallest useful feasibility probe; Path 2 is the target if these interfaces must become training truth. Path 3 is a display aid only. Do not use a smoothed or thresholded proxy to mint `historical_R2`.

## Next experiment and acceptance gates

**Recommended next experiment: a geometry-only paired-interface observability gate using Path 1.** Use one small closed, thin-walled mother with a verified material volume, plus a held-out, measured or explicitly synthetic nonplanar branching interface reference. The reference is for extractor calibration only; it must not be called a simulated or physical fracture. Generate no target fragment count and no production knife-plane geometry. Before running a mechanical case, pass the same point/cell IDs, cell volumes, and interface side labels through the proposed output package and the RelicWeave ingress path. This resolves whether the cell-interface route can produce honest, paired meshes and whether the downstream interface contract is adequate. If there is no verified volume for a real VesselForge mother, stop at a clearly synthetic geometry fixture and record real-mother mapping as blocked by volume ingress.

Predeclare these checks:

1. **Volume and mass:** every material cell/point is assigned exactly once to a fragment or an explicitly declared unresolved/debris set. The sum of fragment volumes/masses agrees with the source material volume/mass within the independently measured volume-meshing tolerance; do not hide missing volume in a target count.
2. **Pair provenance and complementarity:** every internal interface patch has one stable `surface_id`, two distinct `fragment_id`s, explicit sides, units/frame, and source event/bond references. On the reference fixture, the two sides have matched area, coincident material-space vertices within the mesh tolerance, and opposing normals. Do not double-count the common area in labels or energy ledgers. Exterior faces must retain mother-surface provenance rather than becoming cracks.
3. **Manifold/topology:** each fragment mesh is oriented and has no self-intersections or edges with more than two incident faces. Closed fragment shells must be watertight. For an open crack terminating at the exterior, allow only the predeclared crack-front/exterior boundary loops; reject unexplained boundary or non-manifold edges rather than healing them silently.
4. **Resolution, uncertainty, and discretization bias:** report `h`, `delta`, reconstruction scale, and a surface uncertainty band. Re-run the same reference with non-symmetry rotations of the point layout and at least one spacing refinement; compare surface distance, area, topology, and fragment mass to the reference uncertainty. A structured-grid alignment that moves the surface beyond that uncertainty fails. Do not claim roughness below the resolved spacing/horizon scale.
5. **Downstream route:** import the accepted proxy as a fragment asset with interface provenance in the truth/label side; create an observed fragment through the existing observation route; pass only `a12.obs.v1` observation-derived features into candidate ranking. Keep `interface_id`, fragment identity, pose, relation/R2, and event labels outside the ranking feature payload. Until surface provenance is qualified, ranking may consume the observation as geometry, but relation labels remain `UNKNOWN`.

This gate proves geometry serialization, reconstruction error, pairing, and consumer wiring only. A separate physical qualification experiment needs a measured ceramic fracture reference (ideally both mated sides or suitable volumetric imaging), calibrated material and loading, and the same orientation/refinement study. The current free-fracture pilot is not that experiment: its own source review marks load validity pending, material uncalibrated, energy closure diagnostic-only, and geometry analytic.

## Target RelicWeave mapping contract

The proposed downstream sequence is not implemented end-to-end today:

`verified VesselForge solid + surface package → void-safe PG material points/cells → native bond/event history → paired fragment meshes with event/surface provenance → fragment asset → physical/visible observation → observation-only feature bundle → ranked candidates`.

The last stages already have separate data roles: `adapt_observed_features` returns `features`, `labels`, and `provenance` separately and recursively rejects answer-side fields (`relicweave-synlab/synlab/observe/features.py:1-17,166`). The candidate query should be the observed damaged fragment, not a clean PG point set and not its answer-bearing interface labels. A source-reader or metadata hash alone cannot upgrade the input to physical fracture truth; the produced mesh and provenance must be read back and compared row-for-row before training use.

The precise unresolved architectural decision is therefore semantic: **is PG-Frag expected to export a labeled visualization/reconstruction proxy, or to generate explicit simulated crack-surface truth?** If the goal remains material-realistic paired crack geometry for R2 supervision, select Path 2 (or an experimentally grounded fracture source) as the eventual producer. Path 1 can be the next narrow experiment, with `RECONSTRUCTION_PROXY` status throughout; Path 3 must stay display-only. The current repository evidence cannot justify calling any output of the unmodified OSB bond graph physical paired-surface GT.

## Primary references

- [Peridynamics.jl stable API: `Body`, point positions/volumes, and `uniform_box`](https://kaipartmann.github.io/Peridynamics.jl/stable/public_api_reference/). The documented input is a material plus positions and per-point volumes (or an Abaqus input mesh); `uniform_box` is a cuboid point-grid generator, not a VesselForge surface-to-volume adapter.
- [Diehl et al., “Extraction of Fragments and Waves After Impact Damage in Particle-Based Simulations,” 2015, DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-319-51954-8_2), [author PDF](https://freysn.github.io/papers/extraction_peri.pdf). Component-label visualization; thresholds/radius affect labels; particle placement sensitivity is discussed.
- [Giannakeas et al., “Coupling XFEM and Peridynamics for brittle fracture simulation: part II—adaptive relocation strategy,” DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00466-020-01872-8). The paper describes crack-tip estimation from a damage index and notes that bonds do not directly specify crack geometry.
- [Dipasquale et al., “Dependence of crack paths on the orientation of regular 2D peridynamic grids,” DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.engfracmech.2016.03.022). Direct evidence for grid-direction bias in regular peridynamic discretizations.
- [Ai & Augarde, “An adaptive cracking particle method providing explicit and accurate description of 3D crack surfaces,” DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/nme.5786). A researched explicit nonplanar crack-surface tracking formulation; relevant design precedent, not an implementation claim about PG-Frag.
- [Chen et al., “Peridynamics-Based Fracture Animation for Elastoplastic Solids,” DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/cgf.13236). An animation-oriented mesh-embedding approach that explicitly generates crack surfaces; useful as a geometric method precedent, not evidence that the current mechanics kernel has this capability.
