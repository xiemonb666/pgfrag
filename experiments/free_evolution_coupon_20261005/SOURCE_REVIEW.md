## Fixed engineering case

The case uses the native analytic `uniform_box` cell-centre grid with lengths `12 × 9 × 9 mm`. Point spacing `h = 0.6 mm` and horizon `δ = 1.8 mm` are separately stored; `δ/h = 3`. The expected grid is `20 × 15 × 15 = 4,500` points. Using the box's six flat faces and the complete spherical horizon criterion `min_d(L_d/2 − |X_d|) ≥ δ`, there are `14 × 9 × 9 = 1,134` points with an untruncated horizon, or `25.2%`. The x-axis cell centers satisfying `|x| ≤ 4.2 mm` are `±3.9, ±3.3, ±2.7, ±2.1, ±1.5, ±0.9, ±0.3 mm` (14 points); y and z each contribute 9 points. Each one-cell end grip has 225 points. These counts follow from the fixed coordinates and are asserted against the native `uniform_box` readback before integration. The driver also records the three unique-coordinate grid counts and a per-point minimum-face-clearance-minus-`δ` margin, with a histogram in units of `h`.

At uniform point volume `h³`, the declared specimen volume is `9.72 × 10⁻⁷ m³`; with the prototype density `2,100 kg/m³`, the design mass is `0.0020412 kg`. The end-grip centre span is `11.4 mm`, so the selected `10⁻⁴` engineering strain corresponds to `1.14 µm` relative end displacement. This is a small numerical load selected for a reversible algorithm check, not a measured material response.

The material is native `OSBMaterial{NoCorrection}` with `E = 5 GPa`, `ν = 0.24`, `ρ = 2,100 kg/m³`, and `Gc = 45 J/m²`. Every value remains `UNCALIBRATED`. `material!` is followed by `no_failure!`, then the driver verifies failure permission is false for every point. The current fixed choice is `NoCorrection`: native surface truncation remains present and uncorrected. The run cannot qualify surface stiffness, an artifact's physics, crack behavior, contact, or fracture energy.

The left end's xyz velocity is zero. The right end's yz velocities are zero and its x velocity comes from the native `velocity_bc!` API. One continuous cubic smoothstep displacement path is defined directly in physical seconds for ramp, hold, unload, and post-unload dwell. At native boundary time `tₙ`, the prescribed velocity is the secant `(u_target(tₙ) − u_target(tₙ − Δt))/Δt`; the native half-step drift therefore places every driven grip at the continuous path's value at `tₙ`. Changing `Δt` does not stretch the phase durations. The step is capped by the shortest positive phase duration divided by eight, and the total step count is `ceil(total_duration/Δt)` with a 192-step limit. The final solver time can exceed the requested total by less than one step. The discrete sampled maximum grip displacement can be below the continuous peak if no step time falls inside the hold phase. During the post-unload dwell, both grip sets remain held at zero velocity while interior points receive no boundary condition and follow native force/acceleration integration. This is free interior motion with held grips, not an unconstrained-body ringdown. There is no prescribed all-point deformation, precrack, or manual bond edit. Failure is disabled so the case only tests a short reversible elastic response.

## Discrete reactions, work, and energy quantities

The pinned solver does not store a separate support-reaction field. Its `VelocityVerlet` step first predicts `v_half = v_full,previous + a_previous Δt/2`, applies velocity boundary conditions by replacing constrained entries in `velocity_half`, drifts displacement and position, evaluates force density, then updates acceleration and full-step velocity. The recorder therefore stores `U`, full-step `V`, native `velocity_half`, native acceleration, and both force-density fields at every output-hook state. The point-state tensor stores these staggered values with a self-describing sidecar: `U`, full `V`, acceleration, force densities, and OSB energy are at `tₙ`; for `n ≥ 1`, `velocity_half` is centered at `tₙ₋₁/₂` after the BC was evaluated at `tₙ`. The `n = 0` half-velocity is the solver's initialized zero placeholder and its timestamp is recorded as unavailable.

For each grip point and constrained component, the driver computes the actual algorithmic boundary impulse

`J_BC = m (v_half,after_BC − (v_full,previous + a_previous Δt/2))`.

`J_BC/Δt` is reported as the average constraint force on the specimen over the update interval `[tₙ₋₁,tₙ]`; a support force on the grip has the opposite sign. It is a discrete kinematic-constraint impulse, not a continuum endpoint traction. The free-degree check evaluates the same difference where no boundary condition applies and rejects a nonzero impulse beyond a floating-point tolerance. The momentum check compares full-step momentum change with the trapezoidal endpoint impulse of native `(b_int + b_ext)V` plus the recorded boundary impulses, respecting the full-step force and half-step boundary time levels.

The driver reports each constrained velocity replacement's kinetic-energy jump, `½m(v_half,after² − v_half,before²)`, as the discrete boundary kinetic work. It also retains the requested force-displacement trapezoid under a name containing `surrogate`; that value is excluded from the energy residual because the impulse-derived force samples are interval averages, not endpoint reactions. The residual compares native OSB strain energy plus full-step kinetic energy against accumulated half-step boundary kinetic-energy jumps. Because these quantities live at staggered VelocityVerlet time levels, the residual is a diagnostic only and does not establish global energy closure.

At each captured state, native `Peridynamics.export_field(Val(:strain_energy_density), ...)` recomputes the OSB field and the driver integrates `Σ Ψᵢ Vᵢ`. Kinetic energy uses the recorded full-step velocity; mass and total momentum are reported per state. This intact, failure-disabled test has no fracture-dissipation term. A change in stored elastic energy must never be interpreted as fracture dissipation.

The full point history is written as `point_state_history.f64le`, a little-endian Float64 tensor with shape `[21, point_count, state_count]` in Julia column-major order. The 21 ordered values are three components each of displacement, full velocity, native half velocity, acceleration, internal force density, and external force density, followed by OSB strain-energy density, damage, and active-bond count. The last field is Float64 only for a compact homogeneous format; the writer checks every encoded bond count is an exact integer. `point_state_layout.toml` records field names/units/time levels, shape, byte count, and binary SHA-256. `point_reference.csv` maps the 1-based point IDs to coordinates, volume, mass, grip membership, complete-horizon membership, and reference bond count. `energy_reaction_history.csv` is one row per solver state and reports the energy and reaction ledger. This is a diagnostic binary format for this experiment only; it does not change PG-Frag's production result format.
